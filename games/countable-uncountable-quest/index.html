Countable And Uncountable Nouns

Some nouns are things we can count one by one. Other nouns are materials, ideas, or groups that we usually do not count directly.

Teacher question: Can I naturally say one, two, three before this noun? If yes, it is usually countable. If not, it is usually uncountable, and we need words such as some, any, much, or a container phrase like a cup of.

1. Countable nouns

Countable nouns are things we can count. They can be singular or plural.

  • an apple, two apples
  • a chair, many chairs
  • Singular countable nouns usually need a/an, the, my, etc.

2. Uncountable nouns

Uncountable nouns are not usually counted one by one. They do not usually have a plural form.

  • rice, not rices
  • information, not informations
  • furniture, not furnitures

3. A/an, some, and any

Use a/an with singular countable nouns. Use some with plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns in positive sentences. Use any mainly in negatives and questions.

  • There's a man at the door.
  • There are some chairs in the room.
  • There isn't any coffee.

4. How many or how much?

Use how many with countable plural nouns. Use how much with uncountable nouns.

  • How many chairs do we need?
  • How much milk have we got?

5. Much, many, and a lot of

In positive sentences, English often prefers a lot of. In negatives, use not many for countable nouns and not much for uncountable nouns.

  • They have a lot of money.
  • There aren't many potatoes.
  • There isn't much water.

6. Some in offers and requests

In questions, we often use any, but use some when offering, requesting, or expecting the answer to be yes.

  • Do you want some chocolate?
  • Can we have some more chairs, please?
  • Have you got some new glasses? can suggest you expect yes.

7. Count uncountable nouns with containers

When you need a number, add a measure word or container phrase before the uncountable noun.

  • a cup of tea
  • two pieces of advice
  • three bottles of water

8. Learn each noun as a pattern

Some nouns may be countable in one language but uncountable in English. Learn the noun with a model sentence.

  • I need some information.
  • We bought some furniture.
  • She gave me some good advice.
Quick rule: countable nouns can use a/an, plural -s, and how many. Uncountable nouns usually use no plural -s, use some/any, and use how much.
