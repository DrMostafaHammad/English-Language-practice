Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, And Better Simple Sentences

This lesson starts with word type recognition, but the final goal is sentence control. At A2 level, students should not only name the word type. They should use it correctly in a fuller sentence.

Use this question again and again: What job is this word doing in the sentence? A noun often names a person, thing, place, or idea. A verb shows an action or state. An adjective gives extra information about a noun. When students know each job, sentence building becomes much easier.

1. Nouns name people, places, things, or ideas

A noun can be concrete or more abstract. In this game, words like ticket, museum, and homework come from the ESL Lounge A1 list, but the sentences around them are more developed.

  • The museum opened early today.
  • My brother finished his homework after dinner.

2. Verbs show actions or states

Verbs are the engine of the sentence. In A2 work, students need to notice tense, meaning, and where the verb fits.

  • We travel by train in summer.
  • The guide will explain the rule again.

3. Adjectives describe nouns

Adjectives usually come before the noun, or after be. They make sentences more natural and more precise.

  • She wore a warm jacket.
  • The library is quiet in the morning.

4. Stronger A2 sentences add detail

Instead of tiny sentences, A2 students can handle fuller patterns such as subject + verb + object + time/place detail.

  • My cousin will play the guitar after school.
  • The friendly waiter carried our drinks carefully.

5. Building a sentence means choosing the right order

English often follows a clear pattern: subject, verb, object, then extra information like place or time. Some questions in this game ask students to build a sentence from a pool and ignore extra cards.

Source note: the word bank for this game was taken from the ESL Lounge A1 vocabulary list page chosen for this lesson. The challenge level rises through sentence design, not through very rare vocabulary.
