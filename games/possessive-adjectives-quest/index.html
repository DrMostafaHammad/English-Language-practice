Possessive Adjectives

Possessive adjectives show that a noun is connected to a person, people, animal, thing, or place. They are called adjectives because they go with a noun.

The most important rule: a possessive adjective comes before a noun. Say my book, your teacher, his phone, her bag, our class, and their house. Do not put it after the noun.

1. The basic forms

Each subject pronoun has a possessive adjective.

  • I -> my: I like my school.
  • you -> your: Is this your pen?
  • he -> his: Ali has his book.
  • she -> her: Sara has her bag.
  • it -> its: The cat moved its tail.
  • we -> our: We love our city.
  • they -> their: They cleaned their room.

2. Position before the noun

Possessive adjectives always come before the noun they describe.

  • my phone, not phone my
  • her brother, not brother her
  • their teacher, not teacher their

3. They show more than ownership

They can show things we own, but also people, relationships, body parts, and things connected to us.

  • my car = a thing I own
  • her mother = family relationship
  • his hand = body part
  • our team = a group connected to us

4. His or her?

Use his for a male person and her for a female person.

  • Omar forgot his keys.
  • Mona forgot her keys.
  • The noun after it can be singular or plural: his key, his keys.

5. Its has no apostrophe

Use its for animals, objects, places, and organizations when they are not people. Do not add an apostrophe.

  • The dog is eating its food.
  • The company changed its name.
  • It's means it is or it has, not possession.

6. Your can be one person or many people

Your can mean belonging to one person or to a group. Context tells us the meaning.

  • Ahmed, is this your notebook?
  • Class, open your books.

7. Our and their

Use our when the speaker is included. Use their for other people, animals, things, or groups.

  • We finished our homework.
  • The students finished their homework.
  • The birds are in their nest.

8. Possessive adjective or possessive pronoun?

A possessive adjective needs a noun after it. A possessive pronoun stands alone.

  • This is my bag. My needs the noun bag.
  • This bag is mine. Mine stands alone.
  • In this game, practise the adjective forms before nouns.
Quick rule: choose the owner first, then put the possessive adjective directly before the noun: owner + noun. I -> my book, she -> her book, they -> their book.
