Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns show who something belongs to. They replace a noun phrase, so we do not repeat the noun.

Easy rule: possessive adjectives come before a noun, but possessive pronouns stand alone. Say my bag, but say mine when you do not repeat bag.

1. The forms

Learn these pairs.

  • my -> mine
  • your -> yours
  • his -> his
  • her -> hers
  • our -> ours
  • their -> theirs

2. They stand alone

A possessive pronoun does not need a noun after it.

  • This is my bag. This bag is mine.
  • That is your phone. That phone is yours.
  • These are our seats. These seats are ours.

3. Why use them?

We use possessive pronouns to avoid repeating words.

  • Is this your coat? Yes, it is mine.
  • Not: Yes, it is my coat. This is possible, but it repeats coat.

4. No apostrophe

Do not use an apostrophe with possessive pronouns.

  • yours, not your's
  • hers, not her's
  • ours, not our's
  • theirs, not their's

5. His is the same

His can be before a noun or stand alone.

  • This is his book.
  • This book is his.

6. Her or hers?

Use her before a noun. Use hers alone.

  • This is her pencil.
  • This pencil is hers.

7. Its?

At A1, remember this simple point: its usually comes before a noun, like its tail. We do not usually use its alone like mine or yours.

8. Quick check

If there is a noun after the possessive word, use a possessive adjective. If there is no noun after it, use a possessive pronoun.

  • my book -> adjective
  • mine -> pronoun
Final rule: ask, Is there a noun after it? If yes: my, your, her, our, their. If no: mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs.
